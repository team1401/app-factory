## Brainstorm Ideas (Problem-First)

### 1. RecipeCard Pro
**Pain Point:** Professional bakers and pastry chefs waste 15-20 minutes per recipe scaling, and food cost calculations are done in spreadsheets or on paper, leading to pricing errors.

**Target User:** Pastry chefs, bakery owners, culinary instructors

**Frequency:** Daily — every time they develop a new recipe or scale an order for an event

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- Basic recipe calculators (web apps) — only scale ingredients, no cost tracking
- Restaurant POS systems — overkill, not designed for recipe development, clunky mobile UI
- Google Sheets templates — require manual setup, easy to break

**Freemium Path:** Free: up to 25 recipes, basic scaling. Pro ($9/mo): unlimited recipes, cost tracking, ingredient inventory, PDF export for team sharing

**Visual Style Concept:** "Japanese Recipe Index Card" — warm natural paper texture, hand-lettered style headings, ink illustration of ingredient icons (flour sack, whisk, egg, etc.), minimalist layout with single accent color (indigo or terracotta), monospace data values

### 2. ShiftSync
**Pain Point:** Per-diem nurses and therapists who work at multiple facilities spend hours coordinating shift swaps via texts, emails, and paper notes, often missing coverage rules or credential requirements.

**Target User:** Per-diem nurses, travel therapists, locum doctors

**Frequency:** Multiple times per week — shift coverage is constant

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- Shift swapping apps (Kronos, etc.) — designed for single facility, don't handle multi-facility credential tracking
- Facebook groups — unstructured, easy to miss posts, no verification of credentials
- Text chains — chaotic, no record, can't search history

**Freemium Path:** Free: post 1 shift/week, view swaps. Pro ($12/mo): unlimited posts, credential badge verification, multi-facility calendar sync, searchable swap history

**Visual Style Concept:** "Clinical Clipboard" — medical clipboard paper background with ruled lines, die-cut style headers, bold highlighter-style status colors (green=confirmed, yellow=pending, red=urgent), monospaced times and facility codes, clean institutional feel with warmth

### 3. MuseumLog
**Pain Point:** Small museum curators and private collectors track loaned items with paper notebooks or scattered spreadsheets, missing provenance documentation or insurance expiry tracking.

**Target User:** Small museum curators, private collectors, art gallery managers

**Frequency:** Weekly — item check-ins/check-outs, quarterly insurance reviews

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- MuseumLynx, CollectiveAccess — expensive enterprise systems ($500+/yr), overkill for 50-item collection
- Spreadsheets — no photo attachment, no provenance timeline, no insurance tracking
- Paper logs — no search, no backup, easily lost

**Freemium Path:** Free: up to 50 items, basic photo storage. Pro ($15/mo): unlimited items, provenance timeline, insurance expiry alerts, PDF export for loan agreements, multi-user access

**Visual Style Concept:** "Archival Folder" — manila folder beige background, ruled report sheet lines, typewriter-style accession numbers, red rubber-stamp authority marks, formal section notation (§ I, § II), archival photo placeholders with caption areas

### 4. StageLight
**Pain Point:** Small theater lighting designers lose cue notes in text messages, board operators miss last-minute changes, and cue lists get version-controlled chaos.

**Target User:** Amateur theater groups, church tech teams, small community theaters

**Frequency:** Daily during tech week, weekly during performances

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- Lightwright — expensive ($200+), only desktop, no mobile companion
- Simple cue sheets — no real-time sync, no version control
- WhatsApp groups — chaotic, no structured cue format, can't attach timing notes

**Freemium Path:** Free: up to 50 cues, 1 user. Pro ($8/mo): unlimited cues, real-time sync, board operator app, version history, PDF export

**Visual Style Concept:** "Theater Cue Book" — warm parchment cue sheet paper, theater production color codes (GO=green, PAUSE=amber, WARN=red), bold stage lighting terminology (FADER 1, STROBE, CUE 12), spotlights with amber glow effects, Fraunces serif + DM Mono type pairing

### 5. ChemBatch
**Pain Point:** Chemistry lab technicians track reagent batches and expiration dates in paper notebooks, leading to expired chemical usage or duplicated orders.

**Target User:** Academic lab technicians, small-scale chemists, quality control labs

**Frequency:** Daily — receiving chemicals, checking expiration, preparing solutions

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- LIMS systems — $5000+/year, enterprise complexity, overkill for small labs
- Spreadsheets — no expiration alerts, no photo of label, no lot tracking
- Paper notebooks — no search, no backup, can't share with team

**Freemium Path:** Free: up to 25 reagents, basic expiration alerts. Pro ($10/mo): unlimited reagents, barcode scanning, team sharing, PDF safety data sheet attachment, reorder alerts

**Visual Style Concept:** "Laboratory Notebook" — graph paper background (blue grid), handwritten-style experiment headers, chemical formula monospace (Consolas or Courier), hazard diamond icons (NFPA 704), ink-stain watermarks, scientific illustration style

### 6. PrintProof
**Pain Point:** Small commercial printers and print shops manage client proofs via email attachments, WhatsApp, and USB drives — versions get mixed up, approvals are lost in threads.

**Target User:** Small commercial print shops, in-house print departments, freelance printers

**Frequency:** Multiple times per week per job, daily during busy periods

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- Prepress software (Canto, etc.) — expensive ($200+/mo), designed for large operations
- Google Drive — no proof annotation, no client approval workflow, version chaos
- Email attachments — can't annotate, can't track approval state, easily lost

**Freemium Path:** Free: 3 active proofs, basic annotation. Pro ($14/mo): unlimited proofs, client approval workflow, annotation tools, PDF export, email notifications

**Visual Style Concept:** "Print Shop Ticket" — warm industrial paper stock, barcode header strip, ticket perforations, numbered job slots like a ticket queue, orange/red accent for urgency, monospace job numbers and specs, vintage print shop aesthetic

### 7. ToolCart
**Pain Point:** Film crew gaffers and electricians constantly forget gear on set or arrive short on equipment, with no reliable checklist system across multiple productions.

**Target User:** Gaffers, key grips, electricians, camera loaders on film/TV sets

**Frequency:** Weekly per production — load-in before shoot, load-out after

**Existing Solutions & Gaps:**
- General inventory apps — not designed for film gear, no weight/size tracking, no production context
- Paper checklists — no photo reference, no team sync, easily lost on set
- Spreadsheets — not mobile-friendly, no barcode scanning, no production templates

**Freemium Path:** Free: 1 production, up to 100 items. Pro ($11/mo): unlimited productions, barcode scanning, weight calculations, team sync, production templates

**Visual Style Concept:** "Equipment Manifest" — industrial cardboard brown background, typewritten-style manifest headers, perforated ticket edges, orange/red hazard stripe accents, monospace serial numbers and weights, checklist boxes with tactile checkmark animation, vintage film production document style

## Selected Idea: **ShiftSync**

**Why this wins:**
1. **Clear recurring pain** — multi-facility coordination is a daily problem for thousands of per-diem professionals
2. **Large addressable market** — ~1 million per-diem nurses in US alone, plus therapists, locum doctors
3. **Strong network effect** — more users = more useful for everyone (more available shifts, more swap partners)
4. **Clear monetization** — professionals will pay $10-15/mo for something that saves them hours per week
5. **Freemium-friendly** — free tier builds network, paid converts power users
6. **No visual style overlap** — "Clinical Clipboard" aesthetic is fresh (different from Hospital Wall Bulletin which was shift tracking for staff, not per-diem coordination)
7. **Product Hunt ready** — solves a real problem with elegant UX, clear value prop

**Visual Style Differentiation:** ShiftSync = per-diem professional coordination (clinical clipboard with highlighter accents, die-cut feel) vs ShiftPulse = hospital staff shift tracking (institutional wall bulletin, clock countdowns). Both clinical, but different use cases and visual treatments.
