# App Factory — Brainstorm 2026-04-13

## Idea 1: ToneCheck
- **Pain:** Customer support managers at SaaS companies (50–500 employees) have no quick way to audit whether their team's responses match the intended brand voice. They read individual tickets manually and guess at consistency. Brand guidelines docs exist but nobody references them in practice.
- **Who:** Support team leads, QA managers, VP of Support
- **Frequency:** Weekly QA reviews, monthly coaching sessions, constant drip of new hires who don't know the voice yet
- **Existing solutions:** Manual ticket reading, generic sentiment analysis tools (don't understand brand-specific tone), Style guides in Notion/Confluence (passive, never consulted)
- **Why they fall short:** Generic sentiment = positive/negative, not "are we being too formal?" or "does this sound like our brand?" QA by reading tickets doesn't scale past 5 agents.
- **Visual style:** "1950s Hi-Fi Stereo Equipment" — warm walnut veneer console aesthetic, brushed brass accents, analog VU meter readouts for each tone dimension (formality, warmth, urgency, empathy, clarity), rotary knob threshold controls. Mid-century audio equipment design paired with text analysis.

## Idea 2: FormDrill
- **Pain:** Personal trainers manually calculate progressive overload percentages and track volume increases week over week for each client. They scribble numbers in notebooks or use generic spreadsheet templates that don't auto-calculate.
- **Who:** Independent personal trainers and small gym owners
- **Frequency:** Every client session (3–8x/day), weekly program reviews
- **Existing solutions:** Generic workout apps (designed for lifters, not trainers managing multiple clients), Google Sheets templates, paper notebooks
- **Why they fall short:** Apps are self-focused (my workout), not trainer-focused (20 clients' programs). Spreadsheets don't auto-calculate deload weeks or flag stalled progression.
- **Visual style:** "Gym Chalkboard" — dark slate background, chalk-white text, colored chalk accent marks

## Idea 3: RentRoll
- **Pain:** Small landlords (2–10 units) track rent payments across Venmo, Zelle, cash, and text messages. No single source of truth for who paid what, when, for which unit. Come tax time it's a nightmare.
- **Who:** Accidental landlords (inherited a property, rented out old house), side-hustle property owners
- **Frequency:** Monthly rent collection, quarterly tax prep
- **Existing solutions:** QuickBooks (overkill), AppFolio/Buildium (enterprise landlord software, $50+/mo minimum), spreadsheets
- **Why they fall short:** Enterprise landlord tools assume LLC structures and professional management. Too much setup for "I rent out a duplex."
- **Visual style:** "Green Ledger Paper" — classic accounting columnar pad aesthetic

## Idea 4: SubOut
- **Pain:** Freelance videographers share Adobe CC, music licensing, and cloud storage credentials with subcontractors (editors, colorists) for specific projects. When the project ends, they forget to revoke access or can't remember who has what.
- **Who:** Freelance videographers and small production companies (1–5 people, regularly hiring freelance editors/colorists)
- **Frequency:** Every project wrap (2–4x/month), during onboarding new subs
- **Existing solutions:** Password managers (1Password team — overkill for temp subs), shared spreadsheets, just... forgetting
- **Why they fall short:** Password managers are designed for permanent team members, not project-based revolving door collaborators. No concept of "expire access after April 15."
- **Visual style:** "Call Sheet Clipboard" — production call sheet aesthetic with tear-off sheets

## Idea 5: WrenchLog
- **Pain:** Home mechanics working on cars and motorcycles take things apart over weeks/months, then can't remember torque specs, bolt sizes, or what went where during reassembly. Photos help but aren't organized by assembly step.
- **Who:** DIY mechanics, project car/motorcycle restorers
- **Frequency:** Every work session (weekends), reassembly after parts arrive (weeks later)
- **Existing solutions:** Phone camera roll, YouTube comments saying "remember to torque to 25 ft-lbs," Haynes manuals (generic, not your specific disassembly)
- **Why they fall short:** Camera roll has no organization. Manuals tell you factory specs, not YOUR disassembly order or "I stripped this bolt." Nothing tracks your actual progress on YOUR vehicle.
- **Visual style:** "Shop Manual Technical Illustration" — exploded-view diagram aesthetic

## Idea 6: BatchSnap
- **Pain:** Etsy and marketplace sellers shoot hundreds of product photos per session, then spend 1–3 hours renaming files, resizing for different platforms, organizing into listing-ready sets, and writing ALT text before they can upload anything.
- **Who:** Etsy sellers, eBay power sellers, Shopify store owners doing their own photography
- **Frequency:** Weekly photo shoots (3–10 products per shoot)
- **Existing solutions:** Adobe Bridge (heavy), Photo Mechanic (expensive, pro photojournalist tool), manual file management
- **Why they fall short:** Bridge/Photo Mechanic are designed for editorial sports photographers, not someone who needs "rename these 8 photos of a vase into listing-1.jpg through listing-8.jpg and resize to Etsy specs."
- **Visual style:** "Darkroom Contact Sheet" — photography darkroom / contact print aesthetic

## Idea 7: SpellGrid
- **Pain:** TTRPG Game Masters prep monster stat blocks and encounter tables by hand each session — copying stats from PDFs, scribbling HP/AC/initiative onto notepads that get lost between sessions. Digital tools (D&D Beyond, Roll20) are overkill for in-person tabletop games.
- **Who:** TTRPG Game Masters running in-person games (D&D, Pathfinder, generic systems)
- **Frequency:** Every session prep (weekly), during sessions for quick reference
- **Existing solutions:** D&D Beyond (requires digital ecosystem), Roll20 (designed for VTT, not tabletop), index cards (lost, messy)
- **Why they fall short:** Digital tools want you to run the whole game in their platform. Pen-and-paper GMs just want quick stat blocks they can glance at during play, not a full digital character sheet manager.
- **Visual style:** "Ye Olde Bestiary Page" — medieval manuscript bestiary aesthetic

---

## Winner: ToneCheck

**Why this one wins:**
1. **Sharpest problem-solution fit** — every support manager feels this pain, zero good tools exist in the gap between "read tickets manually" and "enterprise QA platform ($500+/mo)"
2. **Clear freemium path** — free: paste text, see tone breakdown. Paid: save brand profile, batch-analyze tickets, team dashboard
3. **Product Hunt appeal** — "brand voice analyzer" is immediately understandable and shareable
4. **Weekly+ usage frequency** — QA reviews happen constantly at growing support teams
5. **The 1950s hi-fi aesthetic is fresh** — nothing in the style log uses analog audio equipment design, walnut/brass material palette, or VU meter visualizations
6. **Technical feasibility** — sentiment/tone analysis can be done client-side with a rules-based scoring system (formality via word length + sentence structure, warmth via pronoun use, urgency via punctuation patterns, empathy via acknowledgment phrases) — no API dependency needed
