# App Factory — 2026-03-24

## Brainstorm: 5 Problem-First Ideas

### 1. TrueHour — Freelance Rate Calculator ✅ SELECTED
**Pain:** Freelancers chronically undercharge because the math is non-obvious. "I want $80K → charge $40/hr" ignores SE tax (15.3%), non-billable admin time (25%+), health insurance, dry months, and overhead. The real answer is usually $90–120/hr.
**Who:** Every employee considering going independent, all 59M US freelancers, new independents setting their first rate.
**Existing tools:** Scattered blog calculators with terrible UX, no tool that shows the full math step-by-step.
**Why picked:** Most universal, most emotionally resonant (money), used weekly when pricing new clients. Clear freemium path. The gap between "what people think they need to charge" and "what they actually need to charge" is enormous — and calculable.

### 2. Gear Checkout Tracker
**Pain:** Makerspaces, climbing gyms, theater departments, and tool libraries use paper sign-out sheets or spreadsheets. Equipment walks away. Nobody knows what's currently out.
**Who:** Makerspace coordinators, equipment managers at universities and nonprofits.
**Existing tools:** Cheqroom ($99/mo — overkill for a volunteer makerspace).
**Why not picked:** More niche, less weekly-use for individuals. TrueHour beats it on universality.

### 3. Series Bible for Web Novelists
**Pain:** Writers publishing 50+ chapters forget details established in chapter 3 (eye color, missing hand, city layout), creating continuity errors readers catch.
**Who:** Royal Road/Wattpad authors, Substack fiction writers.
**Existing tools:** Notion (too generic), nothing purpose-built lightweight.
**Why not picked:** Smaller addressable market than freelance rate.

### 4. Crop Rotation Planner for Market Gardeners
**Pain:** Small-scale vegetable farmers need to track which beds had which plant families last season (prevent club root, pest buildup). Using pen & paper or Excel.
**Who:** 20,000+ CSA operations, homesteaders with serious gardens.
**Existing tools:** Nothing lightweight purpose-built.
**Why not picked:** More seasonal, less year-round use than TrueHour.

### 5. Sprint Retro Board
**Pain:** Engineering teams run retrospectives in Miro (overkill), spreadsheets (boring), or Jamboard (dead). Action items rarely get tracked.
**Who:** Dev teams doing agile sprints.
**Existing tools:** EasyRetro, Retrium — paid friction.
**Why not picked:** More crowded space, TrueHour had stronger individual pain point.

## Selected Build: TrueHour

**Port:** 3239
**URL:** http://100.97.65.28:3239
**Container:** truehour

### Problem
Freelancers don't know what to charge. The intuition is "divide desired income by billable hours" — but this skips:
- Self-employment tax (~15.3% on top of income tax)
- Non-billable admin time (sales, accounting, email = 25%+ of your day)  
- Dry season months (not every week is fully booked)
- Health insurance ($600–800/mo you now pay yourself)
- Business overhead (tools, software, accounting, marketing)
- Savings (no 401K match, no employer HSA contribution)

The result: people charge $50/hr thinking they're making $100K and actually take home $48K while burning out.

### Visual Identity: "HP Financial Calculator"
Inspired by the HP-12C and HP-12CP — the legendary financial calculators used by CFOs, actuaries, real estate investors, and consultants. 

- **Palette:** Near-black chassis (#2E2D2C), amber LED display (#FF8C00 / #1A1200 bg), orange accent keys (#E55A00), gunmetal gray secondary keys
- **Typography:** Share Tech Mono (displays/tape), Oswald (headings), Barlow Condensed (labels), Courier Prime (tape output)
- **Signature Element:** Live calculation tape that reprints on every input change — styled as adding machine paper, showing the full math breakdown section by section (target income → tax gross-up → overhead → real billable hours → minimum rate). Every keystroke updates the tape.
- **Style Genre:** "HP Financial Calculator" — skeuomorphic hardware, warm amber glow, monospace precision

### Features Built
- Annual take-home target input
- Savings rate slider (0–50%)
- Federal effective tax rate slider (10–37%)
- State tax dropdown (10 states pre-loaded)
- Billable hours/week slider
- Vacation weeks slider
- Health insurance, tools, and biz cost inputs
- Dry season buffer slider (what % of capacity goes unbilled)
- Live amber LED display showing minimum rate
- Sub-display showing recommended (+20%) and strong (+40%) rates
- Full calculation tape with 5 sections (§1–§5)
- Three rate option cards (Bare Min / ★ Recommended / Strong)
- Rate verdict: "You're undercharging" with gap dollar/percent
- Four presets (Designer, Developer, Writer, Consultant)
- Summary and Full Calc tape print buttons
- Copy rate card to clipboard button
