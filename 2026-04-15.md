# App Factory Brainstorm — 2026-04-15

## Ideas Considered

### 1. GrindLog ☕ (SELECTED)
- **Pain point:** Specialty coffee home roasters and pro baristas have no quick way to log, compare, and refine their roast profiles and brew recipes across sessions. They resort to messy spreadsheets, paper notebooks, or disparate apps that don't connect roast data to brew outcomes.
- **Target user:** Home coffee roasters (5K–50K enthusiasts on r/roasting, Home-Barista forums), specialty baristas doing QC, café managers running cuppings
- **Frequency:** Every roast session (2–5x/week for enthusiasts), every brew (daily)
- **Existing solutions:** Roast logging apps exist (Artisan, Typica) but are Java desktop apps stuck in 2010. Cropster is $40+/mo enterprise. Nothing bridges roast→brew→taste in a lightweight web app.
- **Why they fall short:** Artisan/Typica are desktop-only, ugly, and focused only on roast curves — no brew recipe linkage. Cropster is overkill and expensive. Spreadsheets don't handle temperature curves or visual comparisons.
- **Freemium path:** Free: 50 roast logs, 3 bean origins. Pro: unlimited logs, side-by-side curve comparison, export PDF, share profiles. $5/mo or $40/yr.
- **Visual style:** Craft Coffee Bag Label — kraft paper brown background with rubber-stamped typography, hand-lettered feel, stamp-circle roast badges, tan stitching borders. Warm, tactile, artisanal.

### 2. RepSet
- **Pain point:** Gym-goers following structured programs (5/3/1, GZCL, nSuns) need a fast, offline-first set logger with auto-progression math. Existing apps are bloated with social feeds, ads, and subscription walls.
- **Target user:** Intermediate lifters on structured programs (r/weightroom, r/Fitness — millions)
- **Frequency:** 3–6x/week
- **Existing solutions:** Strong ($8/mo), JEFIT, Boostcamp. All have social bloat, poor program-building UX, or don't auto-calculate progression.
- **Why they fall short:** Too many features, slow to log a set, no clean auto-progression from week to week.
- **Visual style:** Gym chalkboard — dark slate, chalk-white text, plate-stacking visual metaphors.

### 3. TuningFork
- **Pain point:** Guitar/bass/ukulele techs and luthiers who do their own setups calculate truss rod turns, action heights, and intonation offsets manually or from memory. Mistakes mean buzzes, back-bows, or trips to the shop.
- **Target user:** Luthiers, guitar techs, DIY setup hobbyists (r/Luthier, r/Guitar — huge communities)
- **Frequency:** Every string change or seasonal shift (monthly)
- **Existing solutions:** StewMac has some calculators but they're scattered web pages. No unified setup tool.
- **Why they fall short:** Scattered, not interactive, no save/history.
- **Visual style:** Workbench blueprint — pegboard grid background, pencil-annotation style, brass hardware accents.

### 4. SeamRipper
- **Pain point:** Home and indie garment sewists waste expensive fabric by miscalculating pattern piece layouts for cutting. No fast visual nesting tool exists for domestic cutting plans.
- **Target user:** Garment sewists, indie pattern designers (r/sewing, Seamwork, Grainline communities)
- **Frequency:** Every new project (2–4x/month)
- **Existing solutions:** Pattern cutting software exists (Optitex, Gerber) but is $500+ enterprise. Nothing exists for home sewists.
- **Why they fall short:** Enterprise-only pricing, no hobbyist tier, way too complex.
- **Visual style:** Tailor's cutting mat — green grid background, fabric swatch textures, chalk-mark annotations.

### 5. HiveCheck
- **Pain point:** Backyard and small-scale beekeepers track hive inspections, treatments, mite counts, and queen lineage in scattered notebooks or spreadsheets that don't capture the spatial/visual nature of hive data.
- **Target user:** Backyard beekeepers (1–20 hives), small apiary managers (r/beekeeping — 200K+ members)
- **Frequency:** Weekly during season, monthly in winter
- **Existing solutions:** Beetight (dated web app), Apiary Book (Android-only), spreadsheets
- **Why they fall short:** Beetight hasn't been updated in years. Apiary Book is mobile-only. None offer spatial hive mapping.
- **Visual style:** Naturalist's field journal — honeycomb grid patterns, beeswax yellow accents, botanical illustration style.

### 6. PinQueue
- **Pain point:** CNC plasma and laser cutter operators manually calculate feed rates, kerf widths, pierce delays, and gas pressures for different material thicknesses. Wrong settings = ruined material and wasted time.
- **Target user:** CNC plasma/laser operators, small fab shops, maker space members
- **Frequency:** Every new material or job (daily in shops)
- **Existing solutions:** Machinery's Handbook tables (paper), scattered manufacturer PDFs, Langmuir's cheat sheets
- **Why they fall short:** Not interactive, no save/recall, no material database that combines multiple machine specs.
- **Visual style:** Workshop pegboard — dark background, labeled bins, safety orange and steel blue accents.

### 7. CallSheet
- **Pain point:** Freelance film/video crew members (gaffers, grips, sound mixers, ACs) juggle call times, locations, parking instructions, and wrap reports across multiple concurrent productions. Text messages get buried and cause missed calls.
- **Target user:** Freelance film/video crew (IATSE members, non-union freelancers — tens of thousands)
- **Frequency:** Every shoot day (3–5x/week for working freelancers)
- **Existing solutions:** StudioBinder (production-side, not crew-side), group texts, paper call sheets
- **Why they fall short:** StudioBinder serves the AD/production office, not individual crew members tracking multiple gigs. No crew-centric dashboard exists.
- **Visual style:** Industry call sheet — classic production call sheet layout with highlighter accents, strip labels.

---

## Selection: GrindLog

**Why it wins:**
- Clearest problem-solution fit: no good tool exists in this price/experience range
- Passionate, engaged niche community (r/roasting, Home-Barista, coffee YouTube)
- Weekly+ usage frequency
- Clear freemium monetization at a price point enthusiasts already pay for beans
- Visual style (Craft Coffee Bag Label) is distinctive, not in the style log, and perfectly matches the domain
- Bridges a real workflow gap (roast → brew → taste) that no existing tool covers well
