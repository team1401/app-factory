# App Factory Brainstorm — 2026-04-16

## Brainstorm List

### 1. TuneSheet
- **Pain point:** Piano technicians still log tuning histories, pitch deviations, string conditions, and repair notes in paper notebooks. They visit 4–8 pianos daily and have no fast, mobile-friendly way to recall what a specific piano needed last visit.
- **Target user:** Registered Piano Technicians (RPTs), independent piano tuners (~15K+ in US, many more globally)
- **Frequency:** Multiple times daily, every working day
- **Existing solutions:** Generic field-service CRM (too heavy), paper notebooks (can't search/aggregate), Notes app (no structure). Nothing purpose-built.
- **Visual style concept:** "Player Piano Roll" — warm cream scroll paper, perforated edge details, black note-head accents, mechanical precision typography
- **Freemium:** Free for 10 clients, paid unlimited + appointment reminders + export reports

### 2. FormDrill
- **Pain point:** Athletic coaches draw practice drills on whiteboards and napkins, can't organize or share their drill library. Rebuilding the same drills every season.
- **Target user:** High school and college sports coaches (football, basketball, soccer)
- **Frequency:** Weekly during season
- **Existing solutions:** Full team management platforms (TeamSnap, Hudl — way too much), whiteboard apps (no library structure). Gap: just the drill playbook part.
- **Visual style concept:** "Coaching Clipboard" — white clipboard surface, dry-erase marker accents, field-line grid patterns
- **Freemium:** Free 20 drills, paid unlimited + sharing + season templates

### 3. BenchMark
- **Pain point:** Weightlifting/strength coaches track athlete progression (1RM percentages, weekly volume, deload timing) in spreadsheets that break and can't be viewed mobile during sessions.
- **Target user:** Strength & conditioning coaches at gyms, CrossFit boxes, university programs
- **Frequency:** Every training session (3–6x/week per athlete)
- **Existing solutions:** Consumer apps (Strong, Hevy — designed for self-tracking, not coaching multiple athletes), Google Sheets (no UX for gym floor). Nothing coach-first.
- **Visual style concept:** "Gym Chalk Board" — dark slate surface, chalk-white text, colored tape accent bars
- **Freemium:** Free for 3 athletes, paid unlimited + team management

### 4. TastingNotes
- **Pain point:** Coffee Q-graders and roasters still use paper SCA cupping score sheets. Aggregating scores across sessions, comparing lots, and tracking green coffee inventory is spreadsheet hell.
- **Target user:** Specialty coffee roasters, Q-graders, green coffee buyers
- **Frequency:** Daily during production roasting, weekly for sample evaluation
- **Existing solutions:** Cropster (enterprise, $$$$), paper SCA sheets (no aggregation). No lightweight professional scoring tool.
- **Visual style concept:** "Laboratory Analysis Report" — clean clinical white, specimen-label color coding, precise grid forms
- **Freemium:** Free 50 cuppings, paid unlimited + CSV export + lot tracking
- **Note:** GrindLog already covered coffee hobbyist space — this is the professional scorer angle, but adjacency risk.

### 5. HerdBook
- **Pain point:** Small-to-mid livestock farmers track individual animal health, breeding, and vaccinations on paper or spreadsheets. Can't quickly pull up an animal's history during a vet visit or breeding decision.
- **Target user:** Small-scale livestock farmers (cattle, sheep, goats — <500 head), 4-H/FFA families
- **Frequency:** Weekly for routine care, critical during breeding/calving season
- **Existing solutions:** Enterprise farm management (CattleMax — dated UI, subscription), paper records. Nothing modern and affordable for <500 head.
- **Visual style concept:** "Farm Bureau Ledger" — warm ledger paper, red ink entries, pedigree chart styling, agricultural typography
- **Freemium:** Free 25 animals, paid unlimited + breeding calculator + vet export

### 6. SetCall
- **Pain point:** Theater stage managers cue shows using paper prompt books. Calling 200+ cues from paper while managing comms is error-prone and can't be backed up or shared digitally.
- **Target user:** Professional and community theater stage managers
- **Frequency:** Every performance (8 shows/week on Broadway, 2–4 for community)
- **Existing solutions:** QLab (audio/video playback, not cue calling), paper prompt books (standard). Gap: the actual calling interface.
- **Visual style concept:** "Lighting Console" — dark surface, backlit button grid, go-button green, standby amber
- **Freemium:** Free 1 show, paid unlimited shows + sharing + backup

### 7. InkLine
- **Pain point:** Tattoo artists manage client histories, design approvals, and aftercare follow-ups via Instagram DMs and paper cards. No centralized place to track what was done, when touch-ups are due, or client allergies.
- **Target user:** Independent tattoo artists and studio owners
- **Frequency:** Every client interaction (5–10/day)
- **Existing solutions:** InkBook already covers tattoo studio scheduling — but this is different: artist-side client history and design workflow, not booking. Wait, InkBook is already in the style log. Skip.
- **Replacement: StitchLine** — Seamstresses and alterations shops tracking measurements, fabric inventory, and job progress. Paper measuring tapes and sticky notes are the "system."

---

## Selected: TuneSheet 🎹

**Why it wins:**
1. **Sharpest problem/solution fit** — piano techs literally carry paper notebooks into every job. The gap is obvious and painful.
2. **Underserved niche** — ~15K RPTs in the US, plus thousands more non-registered tuners. Zero modern purpose-built tools.
3. **Daily usage** — 4–8 clients per day, every workday. Highest engagement frequency of all ideas.
4. **Clear freemium** — free tier is genuinely useful (10 clients covers part-time techs), paid unlocks the full business.
5. **Visual identity opportunity** — "Player Piano Roll" aesthetic is unique, distinctive, and directly connected to the domain. Nobody has done this.
6. **Product Hunt appeal** — niche tool for a romanticized profession, beautiful design, clear "why doesn't this exist?" reaction.
7. **No style log overlap** — no music/piano/mechanical aesthetic in the entire log.
