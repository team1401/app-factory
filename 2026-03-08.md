## Why BidStack Won

**The strongest problem/solution fit** in the list:

1. **Universal market** — literally every homeowner who has ever renovated anything has this problem. Kitchens, roofs, HVAC, bathrooms, fences, driveways.
2. **Recurring pain** — people do 2-4 renovation projects per year. They hit this problem every time.
3. **No good existing solution** — the current "solution" is a messy Excel spreadsheet with custom formulas. Nothing purpose-built for consumers comparing contractor bids.
4. **Immediately relatable** — "You got three quotes for your kitchen and trying to figure out which one is actually cheapest" is a one-sentence pitch that makes anyone say "oh yeah, that's annoying."
5. **Clear freemium path** — Free: 1 project at a time. Paid: save multiple projects, PDF export, share with spouse/partner, contractor contact history.
6. **Outside Brian's world** — Nothing to do with RV life, camping, travel, or AI. Pure home improvement utility.
7. **Product Hunt potential** — "Finally, a tool to compare contractor bids properly" is exactly the kind of thing that gets upvoted.

## Design Direction

**Style:** Swiss Transport Wayfinding
**Inspiration:** Unimark International's NYC subway signage system, Swiss Federal Railways timetables, the Helvetica-era functional design movement of the 1960s-70s. These systems had to communicate complex schedule/route information to millions of people with different languages and reading levels — same challenge as comparing complex quotes.

**Palette:**
- Signal Blue `#003087` — header, primary actions
- Swiss Red `#CC0000` — danger/remove actions
- Railway Green `#00843D` — best price highlight (universal "go/best" color)
- Platform Yellow `#F5C800` — primary CTA, notes accent
- Pure White `#FFFFFF` — surfaces
- Ink Black `#111111` — text

**Typography:** Inter at heavy weights (700, 800, 900) — as close to Helvetica Neue Bold as a web font gets

**Signature Element:** The comparison table styled as a transit timetable. Contractor headers have colored line-bar accents (5 unique transit line colors, like London Underground lines). Category separator rows are navy with white uppercase bold text. The lowest bid per row gets a green highlight and a "LOW" badge. Rank badges (1ST, 2ND, 3RD) appear in each contractor header. The recommendation panel slides in with a departure-board animation.

**Anti-sameness:** First Swiss/transit wayfinding design in the log. No dark backgrounds. No gradients. No glassmorphism. No decorative elements — purely functional.
